AI、Bitcoin 与选择的权利

AI, Bitcoin, and the Right to Choose

从 AI 改变劳动与生产能力谈起，讨论产权、Bitcoin 与选择自己生活的权利。

中文译文 · AI 辅助翻译

我支持技术，也想要 AI，但我同样支持拒绝它的权利。人们应该有自由反对 AI、数据中心、自动化，或任何被宣称为不可避免的社会转型。技术并不神圣；选择才是。从根本上说，AI 很容易理解：它吸收人类知识，并越来越多地替代脑力和体力劳动。机器早已超越人的肌肉力量，如今，智能本身也正变得充裕。这并不降低人的价值，却会降低人类在工业生产中所能提供的价值的稀缺性。这是一把双刃剑：仅靠出售劳动来积累资源可能变得更难，而自动化也可能压低智能、制造、交通、能源和日常生活的成本。错误在于认为目标是击败 AI。人的价值从来不等同于他在工业生产中的价值，而未来许多技术进步将越来越多地来自 AI，而不是未借助这些工具的人脑。

这引出了一个更深的问题：人们为什么离开土地？作为放弃独立与自给自足的交换，他们曾被许诺什么？人的需求并没有发生太大变化。我们仍然需要食物、住所、安全、温暖、爱、家庭、友谊、社区、意义和自由。文明让围绕这些需求运转的机制变得复杂得多，但需求本身依然简单。因此，对我来说，钱从来不是最终目的。钱是储存起来的选择。你今天工作，是为了明天能够说不——对一份工作、对社会压力、对家庭期待、对一个政府，或者只是对一种并非由自己选择的生活说不。在追求自己的抱负之前，我接受了社会的一部分期待——教育、事业、收入、成就。那是我的妥协。我尊重那些真正能不在意他人眼光的人，因为在不伤害他人的前提下，按照自己的价值观生活，是自由最清晰的体现之一。

这也是我为什么不在意是否“比所有人都强”。我对自己有很高的要求，但比较是一场不可能赢的游戏。总会有人更富有、更聪明、更有名、更漂亮，或者更成功，而欲望本身没有天然的止境。在结果揭晓之前，天才与疯狂往往看起来相似；历史也常常围绕成功者，倒推写出一个条理清晰的故事。对我来说，智慧不在于击败其他所有人，而在于你是否真的在有限的人生中，得到了自己想要的东西。

正是在这里，Bitcoin 对我的重要性超越了一项普通投资。AI 改变生产能力；Bitcoin 改变所有权的含义。一个社会可以拥有极其强大的生产力，却仍然让普通人无法保存自己的劳动成果。我希望世界各地的人都能拥有不易被没收、被审查或被稀释的财产——一种让普通人能够跨越时间储蓄的电子黄金。最根本的问题不只是不平等，而是一个人能否保住自己挣来的东西。如果没有实质性的产权，技术带来的丰裕不会自动创造自由。人类不只用技术打造产品，也用技术构建制度，而制度决定谁掌握进步带来的利益。

从这个意义上说，Bitcoin 同时是技术、创业和制度建设。历史上，货币曾帮助界定政治与经济共同体；Bitcoin 带来了不同的东西：一个成员资格不主要由地理位置决定的货币网络。不同国家、语言、宗教和文化的人，仍然可以自愿接受同一套货币规则。我相信，在未来五到十年里，它的金融属性会继续催生有意义的全球共同体。宗教本身也曾通过共同的规则与信仰形成跨国共同体；从这个广义的社会学角度看，Bitcoin 或许会越来越像一种建立在数学和博弈论之上的世俗信条：不要拿走属于我的东西，我也不会拿走属于你的东西；保护财产，拒绝任意稀释，并遵守对所有人一视同仁的规则。

两百年后，最让人们震惊的，或许不是人类创造了人工智能。更让他们震惊的，可能是数十亿人曾接受这样一种制度：他们几十年劳动储存下来的价值，可以在未经本人直接同意的情况下被稀释。下一个时代某些最重要的原则，也许会非常简单：保护私有财产，保护言论，保护个人离开的能力。当一个人一生储存的能量不容易被夺走，储蓄才重新有意义。AI 或许会成为人类创造过的最强大的生产技术，但技术不会自动创造良好的文明。AI 可能创造丰裕；Bitcoin 或许能帮助人们拥有它。技术创造力量，但制度决定谁掌握这种力量。这就是为什么，对我来说，我们构建的制度最终可能比机器本身更重要。

I support technology, and I want AI, but I also support the right to reject it. People should be free to oppose AI, data centers, automation, or any social transformation presented as inevitable. Technology is not sacred; choice is. AI is easy to understand at a fundamental level: it absorbs human knowledge and increasingly replaces both intellectual and physical labor. Machines already surpassed human muscle, and now intelligence itself is becoming abundant. That does not reduce human worth, but it does reduce the scarcity of human industrial value. This is a double-edged sword: it may become harder to accumulate resources simply by selling labor, while automation may also compress the cost of intelligence, manufacturing, transportation, energy, and everyday life. The mistake is to think the goal is to beat AI. Human value was never identical to industrial value, and much of future technological progress will increasingly come from AI rather than unaided human brains.

This leads to a deeper question: why did people leave the land, and what were they promised in exchange for independence and self-sufficiency? Human needs have not changed very much. We still need food, shelter, safety, warmth, love, family, friendship, community, meaning, and freedom. Civilization has made the machinery surrounding those needs much more complicated, but the needs themselves remain simple. Money therefore has never been the final objective for me. Money is stored choice. You work today so that tomorrow you can say no—to a job, to social pressure, to family expectations, to a government, or simply to a life you did not choose. Before pursuing my own ambitions, I accepted some of society’s expectations—education, career, income, achievement. That was my compromise. I respect people who can genuinely ignore what others think, because living according to your own values without harming others is one of the clearest forms of freedom.

This is also why I do not care about being “better than everyone else.” I have high standards for myself, but comparison is an unwinnable game. There will always be someone richer, smarter, more famous, more beautiful, or more successful, while desire itself has no natural limit. Genius and madness often look similar before the result is known, and history tends to write coherent stories backward around whoever succeeds. Wisdom, to me, is not about defeating everyone else. It is about whether you actually obtained what you wanted from your limited time in this world.

That is where Bitcoin becomes more important to me than a normal investment. AI changes productive capacity; Bitcoin changes the meaning of ownership. A society can become extraordinarily productive and still leave ordinary people unable to preserve the fruits of their labor. I want people everywhere to have access to property that cannot easily be confiscated, censored, or diluted—a form of electronic gold that allows an ordinary person to save across time. The most fundamental question is not simply inequality, but whether a person can keep what they have earned. Without meaningful property rights, technological abundance does not automatically create freedom. Humanity uses technology not only to build products, but also to build systems, and systems determine who controls the benefits of progress.

In that sense, Bitcoin is technology, entrepreneurship, and institution-building at the same time. Currency has historically helped define political and economic communities; Bitcoin introduces something different: a monetary network whose membership is not primarily determined by geography. Different countries, languages, religions, and cultures can still voluntarily accept the same monetary rules. Over the next 5–10 years, I believe its financial properties will continue creating meaningful global communities. Religion itself has historically formed international communities through shared rules and beliefs, and in that broad sociological sense Bitcoin may increasingly resemble a secular creed built from mathematics and game theory: do not take what is mine, I will not take what is yours; protect property, reject arbitrary dilution, and follow rules that apply equally to everyone.

Two hundred years from now, people may not be most shocked that humanity created artificial intelligence. They may be more shocked that billions of people accepted systems in which the stored value of decades of labor could be diluted without their direct consent. Perhaps some of the most important principles of the next era will be very simple: protect private property, protect speech, and protect the individual’s ability to leave. When the stored energy of a person’s life cannot easily be taken away, saving becomes meaningful again. AI may become the most powerful productive technology humanity has ever created, but technology does not automatically create a good civilization. AI may create abundance; Bitcoin may help people own it. Technology creates power, but institutions determine who controls that power. That is why, to me, the systems we build may ultimately matter even more than the machines.
