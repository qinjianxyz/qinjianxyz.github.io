When the World Turns White

一声天下白

“A young heart’s ambitions should reach the clouds.” At the end of a difficult night, the possibility of beginning again.

LITERARY SOURCE

An Invitation to Wine · excerpt

Li He · Tang dynasty

我有迷魂招不得，

雄鸡一声天下白。

少年心事当拏云，

谁念幽寒坐呜呃。

English reading translation

An AI-assisted reading translation for this site. The Chinese text is the original.

I cannot call my bewildered spirit back.

One crow of the rooster, and the world is light.

A young heart’s ambitions should reach the clouds—

who will heed one sitting in cold obscurity, sighing?

READING THE POEM

The world in the words

Li He’s poem brings drinking and consolation together with frustrated ambition. References to Zhufu Yan and Ma Zhou, figures whose talents were not immediately recognized, open the possibility of a different future. A rooster’s cry turns the scene toward daylight, followed by the call to reach for the clouds. The poem restores courage without promising success.

FROM POEM TO IMAGE

A visual interpretation

The image makes dawn a meeting of colors: a blazing sky and deep blue water converge at the horizon. No human figure carries the role of hero. Light itself becomes the turn from bewilderment toward possibility.

TEXTS & SOURCES

《全唐诗》卷391·全文 / Collected Tang Poems, vol. 391 · full poem ↗

These are the closing four lines of Zhi Jiu Xing, retaining the character 拏. The sunrise over the sea is a contemporary visual interpretation; the poem itself turns on a rooster’s dawn call.
