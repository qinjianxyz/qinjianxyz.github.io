05 / 05 · A home in bloom

Three Blessings, One Peach Tree

桃夭

Blossoms, fruit, and leaves: three stanzas of blessing, and a doorway into a shared life.

LITERARY SOURCE

The Book of Songs · Zhou Nan · Tao Yao

Anonymous · Pre-Qin tradition

桃之夭夭，灼灼其华。

之子于归，宜其室家。

桃之夭夭，有蕡其实。

之子于归，宜其家室。

桃之夭夭，其叶蓁蓁。

之子于归，宜其家人。

English reading translation

An AI-assisted reading translation for this site. The Chinese text is the original.

The peach tree flourishes, its blossoms radiant.

This young woman goes to be married; may she bring harmony to her home.

The peach tree flourishes, its fruit abundant.

This young woman goes to be married; may she bring harmony to her household.

The peach tree flourishes, its leaves thick and green.

This young woman goes to be married; may she live in harmony with her family.

READING THE POEM

The world in the words

A wedding blessing preserved in the Book of Songs, Tao Yao follows a young woman into married life. Its three stanzas turn from peach blossoms to fruit and abundant leaves, each returning to harmony within the household. Its author and precise date are unknown.

FROM POEM TO IMAGE

A visual interpretation

Blossoms, fruit, and leaves appear together, bringing different stages of growth and three stanzas of blessing into one scene. Intertwined trunks and an open doorway suggest companionship and a home taking shape. This is a contemporary reading of the poem, rather than a reconstruction of ancient wedding customs.
