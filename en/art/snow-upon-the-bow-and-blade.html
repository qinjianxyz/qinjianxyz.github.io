01 / 05 · Before the pursuit

Snow Upon the Bow and Blade

雪满弓刀

A moonless night. Geese overhead. Snow gathers on bows and blades before the pursuit begins.

LITERARY SOURCE

Frontier Songs, no. 3

Lu Lun · Tang dynasty

月黑雁飞高，

单于夜遁逃。

欲将轻骑逐，

大雪满弓刀。

English reading translation

An AI-assisted reading translation for this site. The Chinese text is the original.

Moonless night; geese fly high.

The enemy commander slips away in darkness.

Light cavalry are about to give pursuit—

heavy snow covers their bows and blades.

READING THE POEM

The world in the words

The third of Lu Lun’s six frontier songs stops at a moment of suspense. An opposing commander retreats at night; light cavalry prepare to follow. Instead of a clash, the poem ends with snow settling on weapons. Cold, waiting, and the prospect of action become tangible in a single still image.

FROM POEM TO IMAGE

A visual interpretation

The image stays with that pause. A rider in the foreground, the distant column, and birds overhead are joined by falling snow. The darkness makes the figures vulnerable; their composure gives the scene its resolve.

TEXTS & SOURCES

《全唐诗》卷278 / Collected Tang Poems, vol. 278 ↗山东教育出版社·原诗 / Shandong Education Press · poem text ↗

The source transcription writes 鴈; this version uses the common form 雁. The poem names no specific battle and gives no outcome for the pursuit.
